# First Day On The Job

_Sep 15, 2015_

So today was my first day at my internship. Well actually its technically a class and I get credit for it.  It was really rainy today so that was lame, but I found the company just fine and arrived still arrived 20 minutes early.  I had previously had a meeting with one of the employees of the company last week.  My "professor" for this class was there as well. I say professor like that because he's not really teaching, he coordinates the applications and the companies and I send him weekly summaries as well and my bigger mid-term and final report. Regardless, he is a super awesome guy and is super nice.

  

But anyways, the initial meeting was simply to break the ice and sign some papers.  I think it took all of about 15 minutes.  I definitely worried way more than I should have about it.

  

When I arrived to the company, RV CoolingTech, the same woman (her name is Susan) greeted me.  She is really nice and very accommodating. It was only us two there because all the other employees were either in a different country or on vacation. It’s a relatively small company, I think only about 10 employees.  The make ammonium based industrial HVAC (Heating Ventilation and Air Conditioning) units (I learned a lot about what this was all about later in the day, but we will get to that in a sec). She didn’t really do much for me to do at all, really all I did productively was copy some papers for her. 

  

After that, she handed me a short booklet that had an overview of the company and the product that they make.  It was in English and in French, I don’t know why it wasn't in Spanish.  When I was reading it I found some errors.  They were small, and some of them weren't even errors, they were just better ways of saying things. 

  

It was interesting because when I saw the errors, it was pretty obvious that they were a native Spanish speaker and I could definitely see the thought process. Some of the grammar errors come from differences in structure between Spanish and English.   I found it funny because I struggle with those same differences, but just in the other direction!

  

 Susan is currently learning English as her 3rd language, so it helps that she can speak in English to explain some new words to me.  She has been very helpful so far with helping me learn as well.  She speaks very clearly and also very loudly (I think this is just her personality though) and so it is easy for me to understand.  When I learn a new word, she makes me repeat it, or if she corrects my sentence grammar, she will make me repeat it correctly.  I can already tell that this internship will be very beneficial for my Spanish. 

  

After I finished with that booklet, Susan told me that I could go on their website and learn more about their company  and what they do and what they stand for.  This got kind of repetitive and boring pretty quick, but I did learn a lot about HCAV and new laws and regulations. 

  

So the low down is that before about 10 years ago, everyone used chemicals that were terrible for the environment, but were really cheap and really efficient for HVAC.  Then stuff with global warming and ozone depletion happened and regulators are forcing companies to use less ozone harmful chemicals.  Ammonium is one of those chemicals.  Its not harmful to the environment, and its also very efficient.  The only thing is that its very toxic and also explosive.  There has been some incidents in the US in the recent years, but it appears that regulators are starting to crack down on safety and maintenance of these systems. So there is always pros and cons.

  

I don’t think that I will be copying papers all day for the rest of the semester either. I didn't totally understand what she was telling me, but I think I will be working underneath some one else for the rest of the semester.  He currently is in India for something, but will be coming back next week.  I should know more about what my future will look like then.
