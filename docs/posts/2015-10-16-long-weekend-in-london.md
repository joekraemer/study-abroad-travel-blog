This week was a little busy for me with school and applications and errands and such. I had my first actual paper.  But don't freak out because it was in English and only one page long.

  

### **A Few things from this week**

  

Just like my mom at home, I end up helping Begoña with technology.  In the past I have helped her redownload Farmville on her tablet and find songs on her phone.  This week I helped her update her laptop to windows 10 and reconfigure her WiFi repeater.  She calls me "un artista", or an artist.  I like that I can help her and return the favor for all that she does for me.

  

Its impossible to print here! You have to go to a computer and sign into your account in the printer settings.  You have to send it to a certain printer.  Then you have to go up two floors and go into another room.  Then put money on your ID Card and use this credit card machine thing next to the printer to activate it.  Then turn the printer on and change some settings. Then login on the printer and find your print job and then FINALLY you get to print.  Its quite the process and I'm glad that my friend Jill was able to just show me how to do it instead of trying to figure it out.  She told me it took her two hours to print the first time!!

With our ticket we got to have a couple tours.  Ironically they weren't for the castle, but instead for a war sites.  The first was of the war tunnels.  These were originally dug out in the Napoleonic war in the 1700s. 2000 soldiers lived, slept and ate here.  Later in World War I it was deemed unfit for humans and they used it mostly for storage.  

  

![](../public/images/20151011-120024.jpg)

  

A lot of this tour was about the big rescue mission of  Dunkirk, where over 300,000 British and French soldiers were evacuated to Dover to escape the advancing Nazi army.  The tour was very well done with interesting videos and historic recreation of the control rooms and switch boards and telegraph repeaters.  This next picture is from just outside the tunnels where they jut out of the white chalky cliffs.

The next tour was of the underground hospital.  The built this hospital, expecting many casualties in WWII, but they didn't have nearly any at all and so it went generally unused.

We weren't supposed to take pictures, but I did anyways.  Just you lovely people.  One funny story we heard was about the air tubes they used for sending messages around the hospital ( the tubes are like the ones at Walgreen's where they shoot medicine to you next to your car window).  They said that if you heard someone screaming in the hospital, it wasn't because someone was in pain, it was because one of the nurses was playing a prank and shot a dead rat through the tube and onto someones desk.

  
![](../public/images/20151011-143049.jpg)

  

Fun fact: This is the first ever anti aircraft gun ever built.  It was build in 1917.  Dover was being bombed up to 10 times a day.  So they created this gun which shoots little pieces of flak (little pieces of metal) into the air.  With a few guns, you can make a sort of wall of metal debris which will wreck airplanes engines.  So because of this the airplanes will turn away instead of risking damage.  This brought the number of bombings down to zero per day.

### **Interesting Things**

No Central Air  
  

I'm not sure how different this is, or how much they are used here, but I have seen it in a few places. Instead of having central air, they just have this small heater in the ceiling. 

  

![](../public/images/20151014-130435.jpg)

  

Two Faucets

  

I have no idea why they use two faucets, one is for hot and one is for cold. So you either burn your hands, or you get hypothermia.  Its really a dumb system and I saw it pretty frequently in the UK.

  

![](../public/images/20151011-113757.jpg)

  

Keep Left?

  

It was funny to see the keep left sign in the metro because I'm so used to everything being on the right!  In the streets the had words on the ground to remind you which way to look for traffic.  I assume that because people get in accidents because they forget that they drive on the wrong side of the road.
