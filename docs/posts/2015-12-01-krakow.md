### Internship

This was my final week at my internship.  I was enrolled in a class that placed us into a company where we would work for about 10 hours a week for 10 weeks. In addition to this we had 1 hour sessions where we would talk about our experiences and about the differences between Spanish business culture and American business culture.

  

It was an awesome experience and I wish I could work there more.  The actually assignments that I had were typical intern jobs, like putting away folders, entering business card data, relabeling boxes, and scanning documents. However, I did get to do some fun things, like in the beginning of the year I was making calls to tons of international companies.  I was basically asked to solicit, which was annoying to me, because I knew that I was going to annoy the other people, but it was actually pretty interesting.  I talked to companies from all over Europe (Norway, France, Germany, Netherlands, Croatia, Greece, Russia, Sweden, Switzerland, Poland, and tons of other countries).  Some times it was difficult to communicate with them because their English isn't the greatest, but we were always able to communicate.  

  

Aside from the actual work, just being in the company was the most beneficial thing.  I was able to experience the environment first hand.  I was able to talk with everyone there and meet the clients that came in.  I was able to see how international businesses work. Also, I was spending time with more native speakers which is always a good thing.  My boss, Susana helped me a ton with new words and pronunciation.  In turn I was able to help her with English!

  

All in all the internship class was awesome and I'm so glad that I was able to do that.

I'm just going to high light some things from the tour.  So Krakow used to be the capital city as well as a very important trading city. They had this one, which they used for food and livestock and day to day things.  But the vendors who sold more expensive things like gold, silk gems, spices and furs didn't want to be by the smelly local market.  So they built two squares. One for luxury goods and one for day to day things.

  

![](../public/images/20151120-101656.jpg)

  

This building right here is called the Cloth hall and is one of the old trading locations.  Nowadays, its mostly just a place for tourists to spend money.

  

![](../public/images/20151120-113421.jpg)  

  

Here's a photosphere that I took from the side of this building so you can get a better view.

  

[Main Square Photosphere](https://goo.gl/maps/5yhmsB8AP1o)

  

Also in that photosphere, you might have seen this tower.  This is the Town Hall Tower and its actually leaning over a little bit.  Wayyyy back when, there used to be a whole town hall and a grainery attached to this tower.  Then in the 20th Century they destroyed the grainery but left the rest of the town hall.  But they messed up when during the demolition and broke the foundation of the city hall.  So then they had to destroy that too! But then when they were destroying city hall, they didn't realize that the city hall tower was leaning up against it.  So they had to reinforce the tower so it didn't fall.

  

![](http://cdn3.vtourist.com/19/6705100-Town_Hall_Tower_Krakow.jpg?version=2)

  

This next picture is a model of the old grainery and town hall.  The square building is the grainery and the triangular part is the city hall and then the tower is obviously what is left there today.

  

![](../public/images/20151120-114125.jpg)

  

On the other side of the main plaza was this giant cathedral called St. Mary's Basilica.  It was originally build in the 13th century, but during the Mongol invasion it was burned down.  However it was reconstructed because they thought that Krakow was too important. Every hour there is a trumpet player who plays a little song that was actually a signal, but the signal gets cut off suddenly at the end to commemorate the 13th century trumpeter that was shot in the throat with an arrow while playing the signal.

We ended our tour at the Wawel Castle.  It built up on a hill in the 13th century, but it was also burned down by the Mongols.  They rebuilt in the 14th Century along with this church.  This church is pretty famous in Poland and there is a ton of famous people buried here.

Here is the dragon of Krakow. Well I guess its actually a statue.

  

![](https://rememberingletters.files.wordpress.com/2012/06/poland-krakow-wawels-dragon.jpg)

  

 There is a legend that says that there was a dragon that would terrorize the land, killing people and burning the villages.  The only way to stop him was to sacrifice a virgin woman. Many men tried to kill the dragon but none could.  Soon there was no women left to sacrifice expect for the kings daughter, He said that any man who kills the dragon could marry his daughter.  So a shoemaker came up with a plan to feed the dragon a lamb stuffed with sulfur.  The dragon got very thirsty and drank all of the water from the river.  He drank so much that the dragon exploded. So the shoemaker married the daughter and they lived together in peace.

 Our second tour of the day was of the Jewish ghetto.  The tour started off and the guide gave us a little history on the town of Kazimierz.  He told us that the town was created by the King Kazimierz III (cleverly named after himself). The King allowed for his Queen's people ( the Jews to come to Poland and many went to Kazimierz.  It was sort of like a haven for them.  
  

![](../public/images/20151120-145331.jpg)

  

During the war they were crowded into a part of the town of Kazimierz which is now colloquially called the Jewish Ghetto. Many families were forced into small apartments, sometimes 4 families in one.  But that was only temporary until they were sent to concentration camps.  There were 65,000 Jews that lived here, but after the war there were only 500.

### Auschwitz

  

 So the next morning we woke up pretty early to catch the bus to Auschwitz and it took about 1.5 hours to get there.  When we got off it was cold and drizzling. We had a 6 hour tour of the whole complex.  It sounds really long, but it was definitely necessary.  Anything shorter and you would miss so much. We spend 3 hours in Auschwitz I and 3 hours in Auschwitz II (Bercanau).  In general, the tour was a hard thing to describe.  It was incredible, but sobering to see everything first hand.

  

![](../public/images/20151121-091148.jpg)

This was the bathroom there.  It was actually a really good place for the prisoners.  The toilets were public, but the officers usually didn't follow them in and so the prisons could plan escapes.  Also, the people who emptied the toilets had an great job. They were able to work under a roof and they were generally unsupervised.  Because their clothes were smelly, the officials wouldn't want to talk to them and so they stayed away. This allowed them to smuggle documents and things from outside underneath their clothes.  Because they brought waste outside of the camp, they could have contact with the outside world (perhaps a local farmer or villager)

  

![](../public/images/20151121-125028.jpg)

  

This is a replication of the in side of one of the barracks. About 700 people in each barracks and usually 5 to a bed.

  

![](../public/images/20151121-131429.jpg)

  

A daily itinerary would look like this:

  

Woke up. Went to bathrooms. Had small coffee. Went to roll call. Went to work. Had a liter of soup. Back to work. Then roll call again. Then they got some bread. Went to sleep.
