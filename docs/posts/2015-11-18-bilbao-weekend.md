So this next one is mussels with rice, which is interesting because I have a shellfish allergy. However, I ate it anyways because I'm young and stupid.  When I came home one day, Begoña showed me the dish very proudly telling me it was one of her favorites and that she couldn't wait to see what I thought.  I really did want to try it, and so I decided to google "can I eat mussels with a shellfish allergy".  Great search terms right?? Well, it turns out that there are two categories of shellfish: crustacea and mollusks.  Custacea is crabs, shrimp, lobster and mollusks is squid, octopus, mussels, clams, scallops. I figured that because I was fine when I tried octopus and squid, I should be okay with mussels.  So I ate it, and here I am typing this blog!

  

![](../public/images/20151108-161105.jpg)

  
One random thing real quick, there are always pigeons in our school.  They tend to leave the doors wide open.  Its not really a big deal because the weather is always so nice, but the pigeons get in to scavange for food. Its kinda startling when they fly over your head in the hallways.  I took this picture from the schools little cafe.  They were inside eating food, but I didn't notice them until they flew right past my face.

### Hair Cut

  

On Monday, I finally decided that I had time to go get my haircut.  My friend Matt told me about this awesome Brazilian guy by his house that cut hair for 10  
Euro.  So Matt showed me were it was on a map and I headed over there.  When I got there I saw a barber shop and so I walked right in.  As soon as I sat down inside I realized I was in the wrong spot.  Everything was in Arabic, and the barber was definitely not Brazilian. The guying getting his hair cut and the barber were both Middle Eastern and speaking what I assumed to be Arabic.  After I sat down, I couldn't really just get up and leave, so I decided to stay.  
  
The barber was actually quite the character, he was wearing a white track suit and had one pant leg rolled up to his knee.  He was also wearing a sideways flat brim hat and had a very full beard.  However, he spoke enough Spanish so that I could tell him what I wanted.  Now, it didn't come out exactly how I wanted, but in the end it only cost 8 Euro.  Who can argue with an 8 Euro haircut?

### Zuricalday

  

So just like the weeks before, I went to the Zuricalday bakery on Tuesday for half priced pastries.  
  

![](../public/images/20151110-113201.jpg)

  
  I had a little long John donut, but it was stuffed with pudding.  and then a "bollo de mantequilla" which means "butter bun".  Its a sweet bread with sugar on top and butter in between the two slices, but its not actually butter.  Its also egg whites, sugar, flour, and milk.  This is a very typical Basque pastry and you can't get it anywhere else in Spain.  
  

![](../public/images/20151110-114612.jpg)

### Thursday Night Adventures

  

So when I was running along the cliff in Getxo that goes along the bay, I noticed that there was this big pier down below.  The pier is at the very edge of the bay, and the cliff is probably 200 ft high, but there is a big switchback logging thing that leads to the water.  I thought it would be pretty cool to go out there and so I asked my friend Alex to go with me.  
  
I really wish I had pictures to show you guys what it was like, but this is the best one I have right now.  I´m going to go back in the day light to show you guys a little better.  
  
  
  
But anyways, so we started making our way down the switch back.  It was very large and beaten down. You could have easily driven a car down it, until we got to the second half.  The second part was much less worn, pretty muddy and about half the size. Then it suddenly stopped.  There was a pretty steep slippery rocky slope that we needed to go down. Luckily some one left a rope for us to use to get down.  
  

![](../public/images/20151112-202349.jpg)

  

Once we got to the pier, we realized that they were kind of like giant cinder blocks.  They were about 8 or 9 feet tall and about 15 or 20 ft wide.  Each one had two giant holes in the middle.  It would have been hard to get up on top, but people had left pieces of wood that we used as ramps and step ladders to get to the top.  The scariest part was shimmying down this skinny path. 

  

![](../public/images/20151112-214101.jpg)

  

When we finally got on top, we walked to the end of the pier, avoiding those big holes along the way. At the end we were pretty far out into the ocean and the waves were big and splashing up against the rocks.  It was really peaceful!

There is actually two malls in Bilbao, one in the center of the city and one farther out.  I heard that the one farther out was cheaper and so I decided to check it out.

### Friday night

  

So later that night, I was watching the friendly between Spain and UK.  The announcers were speaking in Spanish, and so it is difficult enough to listen to them because they speak so fast, so when they mentioned the attacks in Paris, it went right over my head. 

  

I wouldn’t have known if my host mom wasn’t in the next room.  When she heard that, she whipped her head around and asked me in Spanish “Attacks in Paris??”.  So we switched to the news and watched everything unfold.  It was kind of scary because I knew so many people that were visiting Paris that weekend.  

  

There was 12 in Paris, and another one of my friends in Lourdes which is a southern city in France.  I immediately started texting everyone, and some of them didn't even know what happened.  I contacted everyone within an hour, but we kept talking for most of that night.  We were a little worried because France said they were going to close their borders, but everyone was able to return home without problems.

  

  

Talking to them the next few days were interesting.  We found out that about 5 of them were only a couple blocks away from the restaurant.  Most of my friends went and paid their respects.  They said that nearly everything was closed, and so they mostly just walked around and took in the city.
