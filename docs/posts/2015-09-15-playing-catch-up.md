# Playing Catch Up

_Sep 15, 2015_

So. A lot has happened, but I have been busy and a little lazy and haven't been keeping up with this blog.  So this post is going to be quite lengthy. I'm going to try and cover about a week and a half of content, so be prepared! 

  

So where  I left off last update was with our tour off Bilbao!  This tour was great for me and really opened my eyes to the history and culture of Bilbao and the Basque Country.

  

In this picture below, you can see the river and the Guggenheim Museum that is soooooooo close to my school!! This museum is amazing and I love it so much.  I went on a tour of it recently, and I am going to make a post dedicated to just the museum because its that awesome.

So I took that first picture on top of this bridge that goes across the river and connects the two sides of Bilbao.  Its right in front of the university and connects it to the downtown area.  I went back later and took a photosphere on top of that bridge. For those of you who don't know, a photo sphere is kind of like a panoramic picture except instead of it being in a circle, its a sphere!! Its kind of like Google Earth when you can go on the street level and look around and stuff. You can click and drag to move around and up and down and explore. Here is the link to the one that I took on the bridge.

The things that will stick out are the school (which is what you are pointed at initially), and the Guggenheim. But also notice the Deusto sign on the silver building that is right off the end of the bridge on the opposite side of the river as the rest of the University. That is the new library.  Next to this you will see the Iberdrola tower. This is the utility company of most of Spain. Originally, to me the tower was obtrusive and annoying because it disrupted the old historic feel of Bilbao, but its starting to grow on me. I like that its curved and full of glass. It doesn't feel as industrial as some skyscrapers in Chicago and so that helps it blend more with the city.

  

This is the spot where soccer was first played in Bilbao. This plaque is right along the river and very close to the Guggenheim museum. The plaque is a poem that talks about how the workers would play during breaks and after shifts.  It says that sometimes the ball would go into the river and they would throw rocks to make ripples to push the ball back towards them.

One day we decided to go up the mountain side to see the city from up there.  It was an amazing view.  Sadly it was cloudy, but that's how it is here most of the time unfortunately.   I really want to go back up and see the city at night. On Friday, I was writing postcards at my desk, and I put two arms on the desk, and then the back two wheels broke off! I freaked out quite a bit and I was angry that I broke it.  I looked down at the wheels and noticed that the piece of wood it was mounted to, was too small. Thus each wheel only could fit two screws when each one needed four. 

  

The next day, I let Begoña know that I had broken it. I was a little afraid to tell her and kinda embaressed I did it.  When I told her, she told me that she knew it was unstable and that she knew it was going to break.  She said it wasn't a big deal and that she had a board she wanted to use to fix it.  I said "Okay! Let's do it tomorrow!". She laughed and asked me if I was serious. Of course I was serious! So most of Sunday went towards fixing this desk. 

  

It was definitely an interesting experience.  I learned a ton of new words and taught Begoña some new tricks.  As you can see, we ended up doing all of the woodwork in her small kitchen.  It was kinda cramped which made it pretty unique.  I've never cut a board that was halfway out of a kitchen window, and then had a 60 year old woman sitting on the counter to keep it from moving.  It was a new experience for sure.

One of my favorite things that happened this week was when we went to Puente Colgante.  Its definitely top 3 favorite things of this city.  I love how big and outrageous it is.  It is such an interesting take on the problem of boat and car traffic.  The bridge has been around forever, 1893 actually.  The technical name is transporter bridge and there are only 12 still running in the world.  It was partially destroyed by Franko during the Spanish Civil War, but they fixed it up and its as good as new!
