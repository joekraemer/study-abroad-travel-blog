### Thanksgiving

  
  

  

So I am not someone to get home sick, like I never get homesick.  However, I knew that Thanksgiving was going to be one of those days.  Even during the week building up to Thanksgiving I was feeling homesick. 

  

On Thanksgiving our study abroad office put on a Thanksgiving dinner for all of us, which was super nice of them.  A few weeks before Itziar (one of the teachers) sent out an email asking people if they would like to help out on a little Thanksgiving committee.  I thought we would help organize or give sugestions for food or something, so I decided to help out, but I wasn’t able to make the meeting time because I had class.  So she said I could stop by her office after class one day, but I forgot to go. Whooooops!

  

Even though I didn’t go they planned a pick up touch football game for before the dinner and they probably did other things, but I didn't hear about them and I don't know exactly because I didn't go!.  Is any Thanksgiving truly an American Thanksgiving without a little football before? Everyone was pretty excited about this. 

  

A few days later, I was walking out of class and one of my teachers said to me “I can’t wait to hear your speech!” I kinda was confused and I didn’t really think anything of it. Then the next day, I was talking with one of my friends and she said “So I hear you’re giving a speech?” I was kind of taken a back and said “What are you talking about?” She replied “Ya! Itizar (the organizer) was talking about it in class today”. So at this point I was a little freaked out. I sent her an email asking about the speech situation, and she replied “Yup, you’re giving a speech!”

  

I don’t really know why I didn’t back out of it, but for whatever reason I decided to go with it. I didn’t have time to write anything before because I had a pretty big paper that was due the day before.  So I wrote it on the metro ride to dinner.  But hey!  It went pretty well, I didn’t go with the script, but I didn’t make a fool of myself!

After Salamanca, I took the wheel and we headed for Porto, Portugal!  One of the weird things about driving here is the borders.  Because it’s the EU, the basically don’t exist.  If fact if there wasn’t a sign that said welcome to Portugal (in Portuguese of course) you would never know when you crossed the border.  So the tolls here are a little different than what I’m used to.  They have some open road electronic tolling, but the do the cash tolls a little differently. 

  

So when you enter the toll way, you grab a ticket and then when you leave the toll way, you stick the ticket in the machine and it charges you for distance.  This is a cool system and it makes things go pretty quick, but the only problem was that I totally didn’t understand this right away.  So when we got to the first booth, I was following another car and he was obviously Portuguese because he went through the electronic one. I followed him through the electronic one, but I didn’t know that I needed the ticket from the cash booth.

  

So then fast forward 200 km (look at me so European with my measurements).  I went to leave the highway and I went through the cash booth and the lady was like “oh you don’t have a ticket? So that’s a 50 Euro fine on top of the tolls”.  So that was bogus. 

  

At the end of it all we finally made it to Porto at about 12:00 AM.  We were kinda worried about finding parking, but there were plenty of signs and it was relatively easy.  The worst part was that some of the streets in Porto were SUPER small! I got stuck in one of them because I tried to make a left turn, from one of those super skinny streets onto another one.  It probably would have been fine, but people were parked on the sides of both of the streets so I had inches on either side.  To make it even better, there was tons of people walking around, because 12:00 means that people are heading out to go to the clubs.  I ended up having to back out of the whole street, but we made it with not a scratch on the BMW.

After that it was basically dark. We wandered back to our hostel and did some souvenir shopping along the way.  
  

We didn’t much more time awake because we had to get up early.  The garage where we parked our car was open 24H except for Sunday.  Its open until 7:00 AM.  So if we wanted to leave, we had to be there before then. 

  

All in all, it was a great day! It was pretty packed, but you need to do that if you only have one day in a city.  I really would have liked to spend at least one more day to be able to go inside of some of the buildings and museums that we saw. 

The city was nice and clean and there wasn’t really anyone there.  Both tourists and inhabitants.  In some of the pictures , you can see that there are some buildings along the river that are just completely gutted.  It’s just concrete pillars. Those lots should be full, its prime real estate along the river!

  

Portuguese is actually pretty mutually intelligible with Spanish.  It sounds like a French guy is trying to pronounce Spanish words.  So it’s a little less harsh and they have more “sh” sounds. 

  

But when we were talking with the attendant at the parking lot, we were able to understand each other enough.  He was able to tell me that even though the parking lot is usually by hour, some attendants will give you a deal if you are parking for longer than a day.  So it was a pretty complex idea, but with Portuguese and some hand motions, I was able to understand.
