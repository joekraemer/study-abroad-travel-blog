# New Foods and a Weekend to Madrid and Toledo

_Sep 30, 2015_

This post is long. I'm not gunna lie to you, but if you just wanna read parts of it, there is a section on foods I've been eating here, the Athletic Bilbao vs Real Madrid game, and also on my weekend trip to Madrid and Toledo.  Nevertheless, we will start from the beginning

  

So on Sunday, we were put into contact with a girl named Bri.  She studied here in Bilbao last semester and graduated last May.  Her previous host family did not have a student this semester and asked Bri if she wanted to return and stay with them for free.  Of course she accepted and so now she is here!

  

It’s been really nice to talk to her.  She has a lot off advice and recommendations for things to do and places to go.

  

On Tuesday night I we met up in a café in Bilbao to play our trip to London!  It was kind of stressful because the hotels are last minute and a kind of expensive.  However, we got everything done. We won’t be sleeping on benches.  We haven’t made our plans for activities yet in London, but a couple of us planned a day trip to the Dover.  I’m so excited for this! The White Cliffs are so beautiful!

## FOOD

  

So Begoña is a fantastic cook! She cooks basically all of my meals.  The food has been absolutely delicious, and I have been a little adventurous and tried some new foods.  Some of the foods here are Basque and so you won't find them in other parts of Spain.

  

So this meal right here is pretty typical.  You have some amazing sausage on the left.  Flan (its like a slightly harder pudding) is up on the top left.  Tomato salad with plenty of oil and vinegar is on the top right.  Front and center is a "tortilla". Its not a normal tortilla by any means.  Its more like an omelet. It really is an omelet, but it has lots of sliced potatoes in it.  Its awesome!!

  

![](../public/images/20150922-204746.jpg)

  

Here is something that I might have for dinner.  Dinners tend to be small, more like a snack because they have such big lunches.  So this is Iberian ham and some kind of cheese.  The ham is soooooo good! I love it! At first when she put it on my plate I thought it was just uncooked bacon and I was freaking out!

  

![](../public/images/20150916-213548.jpg)

  

I had this for dessert this week. Its called a butter cookie.  Its basically a graham cracker that she put butter in between.  It was kinda gross.

  

![](../public/images/20150927-204849.jpg)

  

Okay, so remember when I told you that I was being adventurous? Here it is.

So you can see that at the bottom is an oil soaked salad.  Its not that bad, you just have to let it drip dry a little.  On the top left is some pretty good fish.  I'm not sure what is was called, she just called it the white fish.  In the top middle, there is some octopus.  Yup, I had octopus.  I was surprised that I went through with eating it, but it actually wasn't that bad, just really salty and chewy. 

  

![](../public/images/20150913-144425.jpg)

  

Second big step: Anchovies. One day my host mom asked me if I liked anchovies and I didn't know what to say, so I just said that I never had tried them.  This was true, but she took it as an invitation to enlighten me.  

  

This is what I was served. Anchovies right out of a tin, served up on some bread.  It was okay, if you haven't had them before, I promise you won't die.  They are just like a really salty fish.

  

![](../public/images/20150912-162520.jpg)

  

I guess she didn't think I had enough, so I had fried anchovies later that week.  These ones were better, they didn't taste as strong because they were fried.  It did sketch me out that I had to pull the tails off though

  

![](../public/images/20150923-150259.jpg)

  

## Athletic vs Real Madrid

  

So as soon as we arrived to Bilbao at the beginning of September, a couple of us had been talking about going to the Real Madrid game.  We didn’t think that we were going to get tickets and we thought that they were going to be very expensive.  However, my friend Alex bought tickets for us for 50 Euro! I thought the tickets were going to be much more expensive and then I wouldn't be able to go!

  

![](../public/images/20150923-203044.jpg)

  

  

The game was great!! It atmosphere was much different than the Augsburg game.  There was a lot of hatred for Ronaldo.  Whenever he was close to the ball, the entire stadium was booing him.  Everyone was very into the game and very emotional about everything.  If a player went down, or there was some kind of penalty, everyone jumped out of their seats and started yelling and swearing at the ref.

  

![](../public/images/20150923-211259.jpg)

  

 It was so much fun.  Even though we lost 1-2, I still went home feeling amazing even though I lost my voice.  It was worth every penny.

  

Side note: This whole sandwich thing at half time is so weird to me.  When I say that “everyone pulls out a sandwich”, I’m not exaggerating. I think like 97% of the crowd brings one. The other 3% are foreigners. It’s ridiculous. You can see in the picture there are like 15 sandwiches, and there are like 10 more that you can’t see. It’s so bizarre.

  

![](../public/images/20150923-214929.jpg)

The walls were riddled with marble statues and large paintings.  In the center was the kings throne.  Two gold lions guarded the throne.  Above the throne was was the Spanish coat of arms.

  

![](../public/images/2000px-Escudo-de-Espa-a-mazonado.svg.png)

  

  

 This has been a symbol of Spain for a long time and also was the reason for the $ symbol.  During the time when Europe was claiming new territories and exploring the world, Spain was very powerful.  Their money was minted with this symbol of two poles with ribbons. Its pretty easy to see how this translated into money symbol.  The two poles became the two vertical bars and the ribbons was represented as that “S”, that goes over them.

The next morning we woke up bright and early to grab a quick breakfast and head out to Toledo. Toledo is an interesting city.  So many different people have influenced this city.  The Jews first settled in the city, then the Romans took over the town, then later the Moors conquered Toledo. The Christians, Muslims and Jews lived in coexistence until the Christians claimed the town.  However, the did not pillage the town.  In fact, the opened translation centers to translate Arab books into Castillian and Latin.

  

  

![](../public/images/20150926-095627.jpg)

  

![](../public/images/20150926-100035.jpg)

  

We met a up with a tour guide inside and immediately headed up a hill for a view of the city.  This view was one of my favorite parts of this trip.  Its absolutely amazing view of this old city. 

  

![](../public/images/20150926-101933.jpg)

  

Aqueducts were used to bring water from the mountains into the city.

  

![](../public/images/20150926-104256.jpg)

  

The tour guide explained to us that there is a law that all buildings must be constructed to fit in.  Only materials like rock, brick, ceramic, and clay can be used.  For example, this quaint house is actually a "discoteca" or a club. 

  

![](../public/images/20150926-104220.jpg)

  

Next we hopped off and toured the 15th Century Toledo Cathedral.  This was so incredible.  From the outside it appears to be incredible, but the inside is just a world of its own.

We went in this tiny room and saw The Great Monstrance of Arfe.  It took 5 years to build it is over 10 feet tall.  The whole thing is made up of over 183 kilos of gold.  Thats over 300 lbs! 

  

![](http://4.bp.blogspot.com/-KvcsZFIe8zw/VghoWIEkajI/AAAAAAAAP08/2LuhZRv-exQ/s400/20150926_115804.jpg)

  

The whole thing can be dismantled because it is made up of 6,00 unscrewable pieces.  This was done twice; once to hide it from Napoleon and another time to protect it during the Spanish Civil War.

  

![](http://2.bp.blogspot.com/-wUGFNVu02uI/Vghoup-kUPI/AAAAAAAAP1Q/PWN4pF7gRhU/s400/20150926_115755.jpg)
